## Common Grammar Issues

### Subject-Verb Agreement
❌ "The team are working hard"
✅ "The team is working hard"

### Verb Tense Consistency
❌ "She walks to the store and bought milk"
✅ "She walks to the store and buys milk"

### Pronoun Reference
❌ "When you call the company, they will help you"
✅ "When you call the company, representatives will help you"

### Sentence Fragments
❌ "Running through the park. Feeling alive."
✅ "Running through the park, she felt alive."

### Run-on Sentences
❌ "She was tired and she wanted to rest so she sat down"
✅ "She was tired and wanted to rest, so she sat down"

## Common Punctuation Issues

### Comma Usage
**Introductory clause**: "After the meeting, we discussed options."
**Series**: "We need paper, pens, and pencils."
**Compound sentence**: "She wanted to leave, but she stayed."

### Semicolons and Colons
**Semicolon**: Connects related independent clauses
**Colon**: Introduces list, explanation, or emphasis

### Apostrophes
**Possessive**: "The book's cover" (singular), "The books' covers" (plural)
**Contraction**: "It's raining" (it is), "Its color is blue" (possessive)

### Dashes
**Em dash**: For emphasis or interruption — like this
**En dash**: For ranges (2020–2024)
**Hyphen**: For compound words (well-known)

### Quotation Marks
**Direct quotes**: "She said, 'Hello.'"
**Dialogue**: New paragraph for each speaker
**Scare quotes**: Use sparingly for terms being questioned

## Spelling and Typos

### Common Misspellings
- Separate (not "seperate")
- Accommodate (not "accomodate")
- Definitely (not "definately")
- Occurred (not "occured")

### Homophones
- Its/it's
- Their/there/they're
- To/too/two
- Which/witch
- Your/you're

### Technical Terms
- Verify spelling of specialized terms
- Check consistency throughout
- Confirm capitalization

## Proofreading Strategies

### Multiple Read-Throughs
1. **Read for flow**: Overall readability
2. **Read for grammar**: Grammar and construction
3. **Read for punctuation**: Punctuation and formatting
4. **Read for spelling**: Typos and misspellings
5. **Read for consistency**: Terminology and style

### Read Aloud
- Hear awkward phrasing your eyes miss
- Catch choppy sentences
- Identify rhythm issues
- Notice repetition

### Slow Reading
- Read word by word
- Consciously process each word
- Catch typos and errors
- Verify punctuation

### Reading Backwards (for typos)
- Eliminates meaning, focuses on spelling
- Catches typos you'd otherwise miss
- Helps with consistency
- Slow, deliberate pace

## Level of Copyediting

### Light Copy Edit
- Catch obvious errors
- Verify spelling and basic grammar
- Time: 15-30 minutes per chapter
- Best for: Clean first drafts

### Standard Copy Edit
- Correct all grammar and punctuation
- Ensure consistency
- Minor rewording for clarity
- Time: 30-60 minutes per chapter
- Best for: Most books

### Heavy Copy Edit
- Grammar and punctuation corrections
- Significant rewording for clarity
- Style improvements
- Consistency throughout
- Time: 60-90+ minutes per chapter
- Best for: Rough drafts needing work
