# Skill: Content Editing

This skill helps you refine prose, improve language, strengthen narrative, and enhance readability.

---

## When to Use This Skill

Use this skill when you need help with:
- Improving sentence construction and clarity
- Strengthening weak passages
- Enhancing language and word choice
- Improving paragraph flow
- Making writing more engaging
- Eliminating wordiness or redundancy

---

## What This Skill Does

### 1. Strengthens Weak Passages
- Identifies sentences that could be improved
- Rewrites for clarity, conciseness, or impact
- Eliminates awkward phrasing
- Enhances readability

### 2. Improves Language
- Suggests better word choices
- Eliminates overused or weak words
- Enhances vocabulary precision
- Creates more vivid descriptions

### 3. Enhances Flow
- Improves transitions between sentences
- Strengthens paragraph coherence
- Eliminates choppy or disconnected sections
- Creates better rhythm and pacing

### 4. Increases Engagement
- Strengthens openings and closings
- Adds vivid detail and description
- Creates more compelling narrative
- Improves overall readability

---

## Common Editing Improvements

### Clarity Issues
**Weak**: "The thing that makes this approach different from others is that it focuses on the person doing it."
**Strong**: "Unlike other approaches, this method prioritizes the individual implementing it."

### Wordiness
**Weak**: "In the event that you find yourself in a situation where you are uncertain..."
**Strong**: "If you're unsure..."

### Weak Verbs
**Weak**: "The presentation was very interesting to the audience."
**Strong**: "The presentation captivated the audience."

### Passive Voice
**Weak**: "The plan was created by Sarah to address the problem."
**Strong**: "Sarah created a plan to address the problem."

### Redundancy
**Weak**: "We need to combine and merge our resources together."
**Strong**: "We need to combine our resources."

### Vague Language
**Weak**: "This is a very good example of how things can work."
**Strong**: "This example demonstrates how the system operates efficiently."

---

## Editing Process

1. **Read the passage**: Understand context and purpose
2. **Identify weaknesses**: Note areas that could improve
3. **Consider options**: Think of stronger alternatives
4. **Maintain voice**: Keep authentic to author's voice
5. **Verify improvement**: Ensure edit is actually better
6. **Explain changes**: Show reasoning if needed

---

## Voice Preservation

**Important**: Editing should enhance, not change, your voice
- Maintain your characteristic style
- Keep your natural vocabulary
- Preserve your unique phrasing
- Enhance without losing authenticity

**Signs the edit lost your voice**:
- It sounds like someone else wrote it
- Word choices are unlike you
- Tone feels different
- It feels formal when you're conversational (or vice versa)

## Sentence-Level Improvements

### Vary Sentence Length
**Repetitive**: "The plan was good. It worked well. People liked it. Results were strong."
**Improved**: "The plan worked. People responded positively, and results exceeded expectations."

### Use Active Voice
**Passive**: "It was believed by many that..."
**Active**: "Many believed that..."

### Show, Don't Tell
**Tell**: "She was angry."
**Show**: "She clenched her fists, jaw tight."

### Use Strong Verbs
**Weak**: "He went quickly to the store."
**Strong**: "He rushed to the store."

### Eliminate Hedge Words
**Weak**: "It seems like maybe we should try..."
**Strong**: "We should try..."

---

## Paragraph-Level Improvements

### Clear Topic Sentences
- Start paragraphs with main idea
- Make the point clear upfront
- Support with details afterward

### Smooth Transitions
- Connect sentences within paragraph
- Connect paragraphs to each other
- Use transition words when helpful (However, Additionally, For example, etc.)

### Paragraph Length
- Vary length for rhythm
- Keep related ideas together
- One main idea per paragraph

### Developing Ideas
- Support claims with evidence
- Provide specific examples
- Don't just state; explain implications

---

## Tips for Effective Content Editing

1. **Take breaks**: Step away before editing
2. **Read aloud**: Your ear catches issues your eyes miss
3. **Edit ruthlessly**: Cut what doesn't serve
4. **Protect voice**: Don't edit into sterility
5. **One pass at a time**: Content, then clarity, then polish
6. **Compare to originals**: Sometimes first instinct is best
7. **Trust your edits**: Once improved, move on

---

## When NOT to Over-Edit

Some things are better left natural:
- Conversational phrasing that feels authentic
- Intentional fragments for effect
- Deliberate repetition for emphasis
- Natural, non-standard constructions that sound like you
